# Cup and Handle

## What it is
A bullish continuation pattern shaped like a tea cup — a rounded bottom (the cup) followed by a smaller consolidation/pullback (the handle). Signals accumulation and a breakout to new highs.

## Detection Rules

### Cup
- Formed by a series of pivots tracing a rounded U-shape (not a sharp V).
- The two rim points (left and right edges) should be at approximately the same price level (within tolerance).
- The cup bottom should represent a significant retracement (38–65%) of the prior uptrend.

### Handle
- A smaller, upward-sloping or sideways consolidation after the right rim.
- Handle retracement: typically 30–50% of the cup depth.
- Handle is shallower and shorter in both time and price than the cup.

### Neckline / Resistance
- The rim level (top of cup) acts as the breakout level.

### Target
- Cup depth (rim − cup bottom) projected upward from the breakout point.

## Status lifecycle
1. **BUILDING** — Cup shape forming via pivot polyline.
2. **WATCHING** — Handle forming after right rim.
3. **CONFIRMED** — Breakout above the rim level.

## Confidence scoring
- Base: 65
- +10 if RSI remains above 50 throughout (bullish bias maintained)
- +10 if volume contracts through handle and expands on breakout

## Elliott wave context
- Cup and Handle often forms at the end of W2 or W4, with the cup as the correction and the handle as a re-test before W3 or W5 launches.

## What to watch for (invalidation)
- Handle drops more than 50% into the cup → failure.
- Breakout occurs on low volume → likely false break.
