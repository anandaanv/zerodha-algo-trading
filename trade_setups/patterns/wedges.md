# Wedges (Rising Wedge / Falling Wedge)

## What it is
A pattern where both the highs and lows slope in the same direction but converge toward an apex. Different from a triangle because both trendlines have directional bias.

## Detection Rules

### Rising Wedge (bearish signal)
- Both highs and lows are rising (positive slope on both trendlines).
- Trendlines converge toward an apex (upper slope > lower slope in magnitude).
- Despite rising price, each rally gets smaller → momentum exhaustion.
- Often forms at Wave 5 or Wave B terminus.

### Falling Wedge (bullish signal)
- Both highs and lows are falling (negative slope on both trendlines).
- Trendlines converge toward an apex (lower slope > upper slope in magnitude).
- Despite falling price, each decline gets smaller → selling exhaustion.
- Often forms at Wave A or Wave C terminus.

### Trendline rules
- Same slope-direction requirement: both positive (rising) or both negative (falling).
- Convergence required: the two trendlines must narrow toward the apex.
- Minimum 8 pivot points, 5-bar duration, same as triangles.

## Status lifecycle
1. **BUILDING** — First converging swings identified.
2. **WATCHING** — Clear converging structure with 4+ confirmed pivot touches.
3. **CONFIRMED** — Breakout in opposite direction to the wedge slope.

## Target
- Measured move = height of the wedge at its widest point, projected from breakout.

## Elliott wave context
- **Rising Wedge**: Terminal pattern — typically Wave 5 of an impulse (ending diagonal) or Wave B of a correction. Breakdown signals reversal.
- **Falling Wedge**: Reversal pattern — typically Wave A/C bottom or W2/W4 completion. Breakout upward signals new impulse.

## Difference from diagonal
- A wedge is a **chart pattern** (price structure visual).
- An **Ending/Leading Diagonal** is the Elliott wave interpretation of the same structure, with strict Fibonacci and wave-rule requirements.
- Both can describe the same price action but from different analytical frameworks.

## What to watch for (invalidation)
- Price breaks out in the direction of the wedge (continuation) — rarer but possible.
- Wedge widens instead of converging.
