# Triangles (Ascending / Descending / Symmetrical)

## What it is
A consolidation pattern where price swings get progressively narrower, forming converging trendlines. Breakout from the apex signals continuation or reversal.

## Detection Rules

### Minimum data required
- At least **8 pivot points** to draw reliable trendlines.
- Pattern duration must be at least **5 bars**.
- Price range (High1 − Low1) must be at least **5%** of High1.

### Trendline construction
- Upper trendline: connects the swing highs (odd-indexed pivots in descending patterns, even in ascending).
- Lower trendline: connects the swing lows.
- Slope difference threshold: **≥ 0.1** (otherwise lines are near-parallel = channel, not triangle).

### Ascending Triangle (bullish)
- Upper trendline: **slope ≈ 0** (flat resistance, max 0.02 slope)
- Lower trendline: **slope > +0.1** (rising support)
- Interpretation: Buyers becoming more aggressive; flat resistance = supply being absorbed.

### Descending Triangle (bearish)
- Upper trendline: **slope < −0.1** (declining resistance)
- Lower trendline: **slope ≈ 0** (flat support, max 0.02 slope)
- Interpretation: Sellers becoming more aggressive; flat support = demand being absorbed.

### Symmetrical Triangle (neutral)
- Upper trendline: slope < 0 (declining)
- Lower trendline: slope > 0 (rising)
- Breakout direction determines bias.

## Status lifecycle
1. **BUILDING** — First two highs and lows identified, trendlines taking shape.
2. **WATCHING** — 4+ pivot points confirming both trendlines.
3. **CONFIRMED** — Price breaks out beyond the apex with volatility expansion.

## Target
- Pattern height at the widest point (leftmost entry) projected from the breakout point.

## Elliott wave context
- Triangle most commonly forms as **Wave 4** (before a final Wave 5 thrust).
- Also common as **Wave B** in a corrective ABC sequence.
- The thrust out of a triangle (Wave 5 or Wave C) is typically equal to the widest part of the triangle.

## What to watch for (invalidation)
- Price breaks out in the opposite direction.
- Breakout on extremely low volume (false break).
- Pattern takes too long to resolve → broadening instead of converging.
